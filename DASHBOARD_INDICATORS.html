BTCUSDT· 15m candles· 3-seed GRU ensemble· 218,515 train / 46,686 test

01 The two heads, and the six horizons

Almost every value on the page is one of two questions asked at one of six distances. Learn these and the rest is arithmetic.

HeadQuestionAnswersBaseline to beat
direction (return_head) Which way does the close move? DOWN NEUTRAL UP ~0.52 majority class
activity (regime_head) Will it move more than this market's own recent average? QUIET ACTIVE 0.50 balanced by design

The six horizons are 15m, 30m, 45m, 60m, 75m and 90m — which is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 candles ahead. The direction head answers all six. The activity head answers four (15m, 30m, 60m, 90m), so a row reading n/a on the activity side is a horizon the model was never trained to answer, not a missing value.

Why the two baselines differ

The direction classes are cut by fixed return thresholds taken from the training split, and those thresholds leave roughly 40% of candles NEUTRAL by construction. So always answering NEUTRAL already scores about 0.52 — a direction accuracy of 0.55 is a real but small edge, not a 55-versus-33 win.

The activity threshold is the frozen train median of a ratio, so the two classes come out 50/50 by construction and 0.50 is the honest number to beat. This is why the activity head is the one the dashboard leads with.

Confidence is not accuracy

Every confidence figure on this page is the model's own output probability for the class it picked. It is not a hit rate and it is not calibrated. A card reading 70.9% confident does not mean it is right 70.9% of the time — for that, look at the scorecards in section 06, which count what actually happened.

04 The price chart

Five things drawn on one time axis. Each is a different kind of claim, and the line style tells you which.

LayerLooks likeWhat it is

This is the model's raw output, and it is measurably worse than predicting no change at every horizon. It is drawn because it is what the model says, not because it is tradeable.

The bright curve above is one window; this is all of them, so the pair shows whether the projections have been tracking price or only looked plausible once.

05 The volume pane

A histogram, aligned to the price chart above it so the two can be read down one vertical line.

One bar per candle, height = that candle's traded volume, colour = the candle's own direction (green if the close was at or above the open, red otherwise). Same 240-candle window and the same time axis as the price chart, which is the only reason it is a separate pane rather than another line: a second price scale would misalign the bars.

It carries no model output. It is there to say whether the move the model just committed to happened on real participation or on nothing.

The three segments are the softmax over DOWN, NEUTRAL and UP, and they sum to 100% on every row. Expect them to sit near one third each: when the widest segment is barely above the others, the model is telling you it has no view, and the right reading is "no call".

The right-hand label repeats the winning class and its percentage — the same number the top card shows for 15m. Bars narrower than 12% are drawn without their number so the text does not overflow its segment.

Only four rows appear (15m, 30m, 60m, 90m) because those are the horizons the activity head was trained on. The trailing active if >x% is the decision threshold made concrete: it is the frozen train-median ratio multiplied by this market's current trailing average absolute move. A candle whose move exceeds that number is labelled ACTIVE.

The threshold moves between refreshes, because the trailing average it is scaled by moves. The ratio is frozen; the percentage is not.

07 The two scorecards

The model graded on its own live calls — the only figures on the page measured on outcomes that did not exist when the call was made.

Each panel is a cumulative hit rate: point n on the x-axis is the accuracy over the first n resolved calls, oldest to newest. Early points swing violently and the line only settles after a few dozen resolutions — which is exactly why it is drawn as a running line instead of printed as one headline number.

PanelBaseline drawnRead it as
live activity scorecard dashed line at 50% The classes are balanced by construction, so 50% is the number to beat. A line above the dashed rule is a real edge; below it, the model is worse than a coin.
live direction scorecard no baseline drawn Direction is 3-class and dominated by NEUTRAL, so its baseline is the majority-class rate (~0.52), not 0.50. No rule is drawn because a 50% line here would flatter it — a raw direction accuracy is not comparable with the activity line above it.

The two heads are never pooled into one blended "accuracy", because their baselines differ and a single number would describe neither. Empty panels mean nothing has resolved yet — calls resolve only when their target candle prints, so this fills in as the server keeps running.

The question this panel answers that a bare accuracy number cannot: how far outside the middle is the model actually willing to commit, and is that move large next to the noise it is sitting in? A tall central bar and a prediction line near zero means the model is correctly declining to guess.

09 Held-out performance

Numbers measured on data the model never trained on — and the baselines they have to beat.

RowMeaning

lift Accuracy minus baseline, in percentage points. Green if positive. The activity lift is the large one — several points over 50%.

the per-horizon table The activity numbers broken out by horizon, each with its own baseline and lift, so a strong 90m result cannot hide a weak 15m one.

Which split you are reading

A green caption means the test split — the 15% of history no threshold, scaler or early-stopping decision ever touched. It is the most honest number on the page.

An amber caption means the validation split, which early stopping and the class weights were chosen against. Treat it as optimistic and do not quote it as a result.

11 The dots on the price chart

Each dot is a call the model already made, drawn at the candle where that call resolved. It is the model's own report card, plotted on the price it was judging.

Position: two states

PositionRuleMeans
below the bar hits × 2 ≥ calls At least half the calls on this candle were right. A tie goes below — 1 of 2 lands here.
above the bar hits × 2 < calls A majority of the calls on this candle were wrong.

Colour: three states

ColourRuleMeans
greenhits = callsEvery call that landed on this candle was right.
amber0 < hits < callsMixed — the two heads disagreed at this candle, and one of them was wrong. This is the state worth stopping on.
redhits = 0Every call that landed on this candle was wrong.

Text: what is written in the dot

●green · below · 1 direction call
A single direction call, and it was right. The glyph stays a filled dot either way — the colour carries the verdict, not the letter.
●red · above · 1 direction call
A single direction call, and it was wrong. Same glyph, opposite meaning — read the colour and the side, not the character.
agreen · below · 1 activity call
a for activity, hit. A lone activity call is the only marker whose text changes with the outcome at one call.
xred · above · 1 activity call
x for a miss. a and x never appear together — an activity call is one or the other.
2/2green · below · 2 calls
Two calls settled on this candle and both were right. The tally appears only when more than one call shares a candle.
1/2amber · below · 2 calls
Two calls, one right. The heads split. Sits below the bar because a tie counts as a pass.
0/2red · above · 2 calls
Two calls, neither right. The worst state a candle can show.
—no marker
Either no call resolved on this candle, or the call is still pending. A call only draws once its target candle has actually printed.

Why one candle can carry two calls

Every new candle issues ten calls: six direction horizons (targets +1 to +6 candles) and four activity horizons (targets +1, +2, +4 and +6). Both heads answer 15m, 30m, 60m and 90m, so four of those ten resolve on a candle another call already claimed.

Those are merged into one dot with a tally rather than dropped. Keeping only one of them would hide the interesting case — the two heads disagreeing at the same candle — and that disagreement is what the amber 1/2 is for.

A tick with no dot at all is normal. The newest calls target candles one to six ahead of the last one in the payload, so they cannot be placed on the axis until those candles print — which is also why a freshly started dashboard shows an empty marker row.
